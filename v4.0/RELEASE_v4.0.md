# ITEA v4.0 — Update: a three-environment architecture

*Theoretical and conceptual documentation. Novelties and main findings.
It contains no tables, no operational data and no detailed numerical results
(see "Access to data and operational information" at the end).*

---

## What the v4.0 update is

ITEA v4.0 is not a change of formulas but an **expansion of scope**. It consolidates the U.S.
framework —stable since v3.0— and extends it to Europe and to a bridging environment, organising
the project into **three environments that share a single construct**: exposure to the
**algorithmic expropriation of labour**, measured in its own unit —the activity/task— rather than
in the occupational title.

The conceptual frame is unchanged. We distinguish **exposure** (how much of the work is reachable
by AI) from **expropriation** (the uncompensated transfer of the professional's intellectual
capital to the organisation's algorithmic assets); we hold the thesis of the **"expropriated yet
retained"** worker —displacement can occur without dismissal—; and we locate the analysis at the
level of the **activity**, not the job, because that is where AI actually operates.

---

## 1. ITEA US — the U.S. baseline (≈ v3.0, with targeted improvements)

The U.S. environment remains, in substance, on the consolidated **v3.0** line: ten complementary
indicators over O\*NET occupations, a composite expropriation index in a dual variant, and
convergent validation against widely used external exposure benchmarks.

The improvement v4.0 brings is one of **validation**, not formula. For the first time the
construct is tested **in its own unit —the task— against an external behavioural criterion**:
**real, observed AI usage** in the market. The finding is sharp and directional: real generative-AI
usage **follows cognitive and codifiable work** and is **blind to classic, routine automation**.
Generative AI is not the old automation: it falls on judgement and knowledge, not on the repetitive
manual task. The result withstands controls with independent external exposure measures and a broad
range of specifications; its magnitude is **modest but robust**, and is reported as such.

To this criterion we add a **cross-provider triangulation** (the main real-usage analyses published
by different platforms), which **converges** on AI operating over information and knowledge
activities present in almost every occupation. And, independently, employment evidence links
**automation** —not collaboration— to job contraction, especially among labour-market entrants. This
is the external empirical support for the expropriation thesis.

## 2. ITEA ESCO (Europe) — v1.2

The major novelty of v4.0 is the **European extension**. Built on the European classification of
skills and occupations (ESCO, on the ISCO-08 axis), ITEA-ESCO v1.2 introduces the **first European
expropriation-exposure index** and a mapping from activity to the European **competence**.

Three advances define it. First, the qualification gradient is **anchored to the European
Qualifications Framework (EQF)**, so that the qualification dimension is no longer a transplant of
the U.S. system but speaks the European language. Second, the European index is **convergently
validated against an independent European criterion**, reaching the intended concordance threshold,
which gives it external standing of its own. Third, a **European behavioural criterion** is built
—real AI usage projected onto the ESCO competence— which did not previously exist publicly.

The European finding is consistent with the framework: real AI usage **concentrates on transversal
and knowledge competences** and **declines on occupation-specific ones**. This connects directly to
the **certification** axis ITEA contemplates —the difference between holding knowledge and
demonstrating proficiency— and to the idea that what is most codifiable is also most expropriable.
The Spanish landing relies on the national catalogue of professional competences as an EQF anchor.

## 3. ITEA Bridge (US–EU) — v1.2

The third environment is the **US–EU bridge** on ISCO-08: a **hybrid-portability** mechanism that
reads the framework across the O\*NET (U.S.) and ESCO (European) worlds without losing the construct.

Its contribution is twofold. On the one hand, it demonstrates **translation invariance**: the
exposure ranking **survives the bridge at every resolution**, with concordance far above chance; the
framework is not an artefact of a particular taxonomy. On the other, it enables a result impossible
without it: the **European exposure structure of ITEA predicts real AI usage in Europe**, a
behavioural external validation on the European axis.

Transparently, its frontier is stated: **fine cross-national invariance** —comparing countries with
autonomous national measurement, where the hypothesis predicts divergence in the detail— requires
native national instances and remains future work, not a closed claim.

## Main findings of v4.0

Beyond each environment, the update yields four cross-cutting conclusions:

- **AI operates over the activity, not the occupation.** Integration is selective: AI penetrates
  specific tasks within jobs, not whole jobs. This validates the shift from occupation to activity
  as the unit of analysis.
- **There is a responsibility axis.** Usage is ordered between **augmentation** (the professional
  retains judgement: decides, iterates, validates) and **automation** (the activity is delegated and
  therefore codified). It is the automation side that is associated with job loss.
- **"Expropriated yet retained."** A professional's value can be transferred to the firm's
  algorithmic assets with no dismissal; the prevailing employment contract does not record that
  transfer.
- **Exposure and expropriation are distinct things**, measured by distinct criteria and kept apart;
  and triangulating real-usage sources with independent employment sources reduces single-platform
  and single-issuer bias.

---

## What is new relative to v3.0

- From **one environment** (United States) to **three** (US / Europe-ESCO / US–EU Bridge) on a
  common ISCO-08 axis.
- **First European index** of expropriation exposure and a US–EU **portability bridge**.
- **Task-level validation** against a **behavioural** criterion (real AI usage), in addition to
  classic convergent validation.
- **European anchoring of qualification** (EQF) and a national substrate for Spain.
